De Morgan's law

Break the line, change the sign. ¬(A ∧ B) becomes ¬A ∨ ¬B: the NOT goes onto each letter and the operator flips. ¬(B ∧ C ∧ D) is not the same as ¬B ∧ ¬C ∧ ¬D.

Try the step checker

The distributive law

(¬A ∨ ¬B) ∧ (¬A ∨ B) becomes ¬A ∨ (¬B ∧ B). The shared term keeps its own operator; it does not swap with the one in the brackets.

K-maps

Write the 1s in. Loop every 1, singles included. Wrap round the edges. Never put a 0 inside a group.

What does a full adder do?

It adds three bits (A, B and the carry in) and outputs a sum and a carry out. Say what goes in and what comes out, not how it is built.

Mark some answers

Reading the result

Read the answer from the S column as S3 S2 S1 S0, most significant bit first. Your table is usually right; the slip is copying it out.

Drawing gates

OCR uses four gates: AND, OR, NOT and XOR. Draw the NOT gate as a symbol; never write a word in its place, and do not use NAND or NOR.

Add a reason after # if you like, for example # De Morgan. A line with an equals sign in it, like B ∨ ¬B = 1, is checked as a fact on its own. You can type ^ for ∧, v for ∨ and ~ for ¬.

Feedback

The checker can tell you whether two lines are equal. It cannot tell you whether you used the right law to get there: a wrong law can land on a right answer by luck. In an exam, name the law for each step.

Tool 4

K-map trainer

Step 1: click cells to place the 1s, then check them. Step 2: switch to grouping, click the cells for a group and add it. Groups can wrap round the edges, so the left and right columns are neighbours, and so are the top and bottom rows.

Tool 5

Adders

A full adder adds three bits: A, B and the carry in from the bit before. It outputs a sum bit and a carry out. A quick check: count the 1s going in and write that number in binary. The right-hand digit is S, the left-hand digit is the carry out.

Before you hand in

Exam checklist

  1. Every simplification shows one law per line, and you can name each law.
  2. When you use De Morgan's law, you have flipped the operator and put a NOT on each letter.
  3. Every truth table column uses every part of its expression. If the question says two things are equivalent, your last two columns match.
  4. Any word like "no", "not" or "isn't" in a scenario has become a NOT gate.
  5. Your circuits only use AND, OR, NOT and XOR, drawn as gate symbols with the inputs labelled.
  6. On a K-map you have written the 1s, looped every 1 in the largest group you can, checked for wrap-round groups, and no group contains a 0.
  7. Each K-map group gives one term, with a NOT on each letter that is 0 for the whole group.
  8. Adder answers say what goes in and what comes out.
  9. Binary results are read S3 S2 S1 S0, and you have checked the answer in denary.
